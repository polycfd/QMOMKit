# Realizability

Realizability asks whether a moment sequence can come from a valid
distribution on a chosen support. For QMOMKit users, this is the question:

_Can these raw moments represent nonnegative distribution mass somewhere in
the allowed phase-space domain?_

Not every list of numbers is a valid moment representation. For example, a
one-dimensional sequence with positive $m_0$ but a negative variance cannot
represent a physical distribution. A sequence may also be
valid on one support but invalid on another; moments that are possible on the
real line may be impossible if the variable must be nonnegative or bounded.

Realizability and quadrature reconstruction answer different questions:

- moment realizability asks, "Can this moment sequence represent a valid
  distribution on the selected support?"
- quadrature reconstruction asks, "Can QMOM or CQMOM construct a finite
  quadrature representation that matches these moments?"

They are separate mathematical operations. A realizability check does not
validate Wheeler inversion, recursive CQMOM reconstruction, requested node
counts, or quadrature topology. Conversely, a reconstruction result reports
whether the requested quadrature could be constructed; it is not a general
support-aware realizability check.

## When realizability matters

Realizability can matter at several points in a QMOMKit workflow:

- initialization from explicit moments;
- moments generated by an external solver or data file;
- source-term updates during phase-space evolution;
- accumulated roundoff or timestep error during evolution;
- enforcing physical supports such as size `>= 0` or a concentration in
  `[0, 1]`.

Distribution-based initialization usually produces consistent moments when
parameters are valid, but explicit moments and evolved moments should still be
checked when the workflow can approach the boundary of moment space.

## Explicit checks

When a one-dimensional coordinate has a known support,
`check_realizability(...)` checks whether a raw moment sequence is consistent
with that support:

```cpp
#include <qmomkit/realizability.hpp>

#include <iostream>

int main()
{
    const qmomkit::MomentSet moments{1.0, 1.0, 2.0, 6.0};

    const qmomkit::RealizabilityResult result =
        qmomkit::check_realizability(
            moments, qmomkit::MomentSupport::nonnegative);

    if (!result.realizable())
    {
        std::cerr << "Moments are not realizable: "
                  << result.message << '\n';
        return 1;
    }

    if (result.on_boundary())
    {
        std::cout << "Moments are realizable on the boundary.\n";
    }
}
```

The result reports:

- `status`: the realizability classification;
- `message`: a human-readable diagnostic;
- `checked_order`: the highest moment order included in the check;
- `failed_matrix`, `failed_matrix_order`, and `minimum_pivot`: diagnostics for
  failed or near-boundary checks.

The most useful statuses for users are:

- `strictly_realizable`: the moments are accepted away from the boundary;
- `boundary`: the moments are accepted but lie on the boundary of moment space;
- `not_realizable`: the moments cannot represent a valid distribution on the
  requested support;
- `invalid_input`: the request itself is invalid, such as empty moments,
  non-finite values, or an invalid bounded interval;
- `numerically_indeterminate`: the classification depends on digits that the
  input moments do not carry, or on an eigenvalue within the tolerance.

`result.realizable()` is true for `strictly_realizable` and `boundary`.
`result.on_boundary()` is true only for boundary states.

## Tolerance and input precision

The check internally tests the moments of the standardized coordinate
$(x - \mu)/\sigma$, where $\mu = m_1/m_0$ is the mean and $\sigma$ about the
standard deviation, scaled by the total number density; the moments you pass
are not changed. Realizability does not change under this change of
coordinate, so the classification depends neither on where the distribution
sits, nor on the unit
of its coordinate, nor on the total number density. `tolerance` is relative to
the standardized moment matrices, and `minimum_pivot` and `tolerance_used`
refer to them.

The check also treats each input moment as known only to its last digit.
For a distribution far from the origin, the raw moments are dominated by
powers of the mean, and their last digits limit how well the standardized
moments are known. An eigenvalue that is zero within this precision counts as
zero, so the sequence is reported as `boundary`; a sequence whose standardized
moments carry no reliable digit is reported as `numerically_indeterminate`.
Moving a sequence away from the origin can thus turn a strictly realizable or
non-realizable classification into `boundary` or `numerically_indeterminate`,
but never into the opposite one.

The resolution therefore depends on how far the mean lies from the origin, in
standard deviations, and on the highest moment order. In double precision,
strictly realizable and non-realizable sequences are resolved up to roughly
$10^7$ standard deviations at order 2, $10^3$ at order 4, $10^2$ at order 6
and 10 to 30 at order 8. Beyond that, expect `boundary` or
`numerically_indeterminate`. If moments are needed far from the origin, a
coordinate measured from a nearby reference point avoids this loss.

An eigenvalue within $1000\,\varepsilon$ times the norm of the tested matrix
counts as zero, whatever `tolerance` requests, so a `tolerance` below
$1000\,\varepsilon \approx 2.2 \times 10^{-13}$ resolves no finer than that.

## Support constraints

Choose the support that matches the physical variable represented by the
moments. The support is part of the modelling assumption: a velocity component,
a particle size, and a bounded concentration can have different valid moment
spaces even when they have the same numerical moment values.

`MomentSupport::real_line` fits coordinates that may take any real value, such
as an unconstrained velocity component:

```cpp
const auto result =
    qmomkit::check_realizability(moments, qmomkit::MomentSupport::real_line);
```

`MomentSupport::nonnegative` fits coordinates that are zero or positive, such
as particle size, mass, or a count-like variable:

```cpp
const auto result =
    qmomkit::check_realizability(moments, qmomkit::MomentSupport::nonnegative);
```

`BoundedInterval` fits coordinates that stay inside a closed interval:

```cpp
const qmomkit::BoundedInterval support{0.0, 1.0};
const auto result = qmomkit::check_realizability(moments, support);
```

For bounded intervals, `lower` must be strictly less than `upper`.

## Reconstruction status is separate

QMOM and CQMOM reconstruction also report whether a requested quadrature was
accepted.

For QMOM:

```cpp
const qmomkit::QmomResult qmom =
    qmomkit::compute_qmom(moments, node_count);

if (!qmom.ok())
{
    std::cerr << "QMOM failed: " << qmom.message << '\n';
}
```

A QMOM result with status `not_realizable` reports that the supplied
one-dimensional moments could not be reconstructed as requested. It does not
replace an explicit realizability check for a chosen support.

For CQMOM:

```cpp
const qmomkit::CqmomResult cqmom = qmomkit::compute_cqmom(cqmom_case);

if (!cqmom.ok())
{
    std::cerr << "CQMOM failed: " << cqmom.message << '\n';
}
```

CQMOM diagnostics can identify the phase-space dimension or conditioning step
where reconstruction failed. They describe recursive quadrature
reconstruction, not multidimensional moment realizability.

Explicit realizability checks are one-dimensional. CQMOM runtime checks use
only the configured coordinate marginals, as described below; they do not
assess the full multidimensional reconstruction.

## Runtime realizability checks

For phase-space evolution, realizability settings check transported raw moments
after moment-source evolution. They are useful when a source update may move
moments outside the allowed moment space.

The configuration form is:

```ini
[time_integration.realizability]
check_mode = final_only
support = nonnegative
accept_boundary = true
tolerance = 1e-12
```

Supported check modes are:

- `none`: do not perform a runtime realizability check;
- `final_only`: check after the complete moment-source integration step;
- `every_stage`: check after each SSP-RK3 moment-source integration stage.

Use `none` for workflows where you only want explicit checks or status
handling. Use `final_only` when you want a practical guard on accepted source
updates. Use `every_stage` when intermediate source-step states must also stay
inside the allowed moment space.

`accept_boundary = true` allows moments classified as `boundary`. Set it to
`false` if your workflow requires strictly realizable moments.

For QMOM, `[time_integration.realizability]` enables checks for QMOM
moment-source SSP-RK3 evolution. These checks apply to the transported moments
after moment-source evolution. They do not apply to QMOM node advection,
quadrature evolution, or reconstructed nodes and weights.

For CQMOM, configure realizability settings for individual phase-space
coordinates with sections such as `[time_integration.realizability.0]`. Each
configured coordinate checks only its one-dimensional marginal moment
sequence: $M[0,\ldots,k_d,\ldots,0]$.


Mixed moments and conditional moments are not checked. The checks also do not
include CQMOM quadrature reconstruction, adaptive topology, or branch counts.
This is intentional: the runtime check is focused on the selected coordinate
marginal. Use CQMOM reconstruction status and diagnostics separately when you
need information about the quadrature result.

## Configuration examples

Use `support = real_line` for unconstrained one-dimensional variables:

```ini
[time_integration.realizability]
check_mode = final_only
support = real_line
```

Use `support = nonnegative` for variables that cannot be negative:

```ini
[time_integration.realizability]
check_mode = final_only
support = nonnegative
accept_boundary = true
```

Use `support = bounded_interval` with bounds for interval-constrained
variables:

```ini
[time_integration.realizability]
check_mode = final_only
support = bounded_interval
lower = 0.0
upper = 1.0
```

Include the applicable form above in the configuration file for the workflow
that performs the runtime realizability check.

## Practical guidance

Start with physically meaningful moments of the number density function (NDF).
$m_0$ should carry the intended total number density. Mixing normalized
probability moments with unnormalized raw moments changes the mathematical
state being checked.

The support should match the variable. Particle sizes and masses are often
nonnegative; volume fractions or normalized coordinates may require a bounded
interval; velocity components may use the real line.

Generate or provide enough moments for the requested quadrature. Realizability
does not replace the QMOM/CQMOM requirement that the moment order must support
the requested node count.

Status values are part of the workflow. Failed reconstruction,
`not_realizable`, `invalid_input`, and `numerically_indeterminate` are signals
to inspect the moment source, timestep size, support choice, or input data
before accepting the state.

Expect boundary states in some physical workflows. A boundary state can be
valid, but it may indicate that the represented distribution has collapsed to
fewer effective points or is close to a constrained support boundary.

## When a check fails

A failed realizability check usually means the moments themselves are wrong
rather than that the check is too strict. [Troubleshooting](troubleshooting.md)
covers how to find the offending moment order, what commonly produces
unrealizable moments, and what to do when a case becomes unrealizable part-way
through a run.
